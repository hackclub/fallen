A journal is a running log of your project's progress. In Fallen, you journal once per session that you work on your project by posting in the [#fallen-journals](https://hackclub.enterprise.slack.com/archives/C0C9B7FHY3A) Slack channel or on [fallen.hackclub.com](https://fallen.hackclub.com/).

Journals are a keepsake of your journey and a way for others to learn from how you built your project.

**They DON'T have to be formal or polished, but they should show what you did, what you tried, and what you accomplished.**

We'll be reading them to review your project! We want to see how you build your project, what you tried, and the late night chaos!

A good journal includes:

1. Date & Title
2. What happened
   - Write this as you work!
3. Pictures! The more the merrier
4. Time spent this session

Both journals below cover the same 6 hours of progress on the project, but with _very_ different amounts of detail!

## A bad journal

<JournalExample images={['journal-board.webp']}>

Made a few fixes, finished my board and finally ordered it!

_Time spent this session: 6 hours_

</JournalExample>

**This journal raises a lot of questions:**

- Why did you do this?
- What fixes? What was wrong before?
- How did you fix it?
- What does the board do?
- Where did you order it and how?
- There are more problems, but you get the point!

## A good journal

<JournalExample images={['journal-closeup-1.webp', 'journal-closeup-2.webp', 'journal-closeup-3.webp', 'journal-board.webp']}>

**Oct 1st: Finishing up + purchasing**

Final checks on my e-paper & BLE PCB badge!

Added stitching vias + repositioned the e-ink connector + widened cutout for the display's flex pcb + added pull up resistors on I2C which I almost forgot!

**Based on a friend's review I changed a few parts:**

- Moved traces so that they're not under the inductor
- Used different zone shapes under the mcu
- Recalculated this trace width (it's a much more manageable thickness now)

Spent so long checking everything for the gazillionth time as I'm terrified I've goofed something up.

And then I proceeded to go to war with JLCPCB.

I was able to carry over most of my selected parts from v1 of this project, except a couple which had the wrong footprint.

I had to update the project, then reupload the gerbers around 7 times oml. but, JLC's BOM tool did not seem to ever update random parts, so I kept on rechecking and updating the selected part for multiple components.

I ended up sleeping at 5am just to get this ordered. Finally done though! 10/10 experience would make a pcb again

_Time spent this session: 6 hours_

</JournalExample>

**The journal:**

- Says what changed
- Says _why_ it changed
- Describes the struggle
- Has plenty of screenshots!
